## What's new in 0.16.0

Still no generative AI: every idea and every pixel is yours.

**Pixel Art, a new tool** for sprites, animations, tilesets and tilemaps, where every stroke lands on whole pixels
- Drawing: pencil (square or round, with Clean lines for tidy 1 px lines), eraser, line with clean angles, curve, rectangle, ellipse, fill, lasso fill, spray, a dithered gradient, an eyedropper and text in any font with hard pixels. Dithering, mirror drawing, seamless (tiling) drawing, a Shade brush that steps along the palette, inks (lock alpha, replace colour, colour only), pen pressure and brushes made from a selection.
- Colour: palettes with a ramp maker, ready-made palettes made for the app, .gpl/.pal/.hex files, a palette from the picture, palette-only and grays modes, reduce to palette, palette variants (try enemy or team colours on the whole animation), and changing a swatch changes every pixel in that colour.
- Selection: rectangle, lasso and magic wand; move, copy and paste, flip, quarter turns, rotate by any angle without blur, scale with hard pixels, outline and wrap shift, on one frame or all of them.
- Layers and animation: blend modes, tracing layers that never export, wide or tall pixels, frames with their own duration, linked frames, tags with forward, reverse or ping-pong, onion skin (up to 4 frames, tinted), a preview window and picking several frames at once.
- Tilesets and maps: draw tiles, then paint a map with them; drawing on the map changes the tile everywhere, or draws new tiles that are added by themselves. Stamps, random tiles, mirrored and turned tiles, tile numbers, a 3 × 3 tile preview, and maps rebuilt from a finished map picture.
- Files: opens .ase/.aseprite, GIF, APNG, PNG, sprite sheets and picture sequences; exports PNG (scaled up with sharp pixels), GIF, sprite sheets with JSON (grid, one row or a row per tag, with a gap), Godot SpriteFrames, extruded tilesets and maps as CSV or JSON.
- Everywhere: sprites play on characters, enemies, towns, items, abilities and achievements; tilesets go to Level Layout and stay up to date (and Level Layout's Edit in Pixel Art opens them here); maps become levels; frames and animations go to the Moodboard, Design Language, Asset Pool and Storyboard; Draw sends sketches here to trace.

**Level Layout**
- Select and move replaces the Room tool: drag a rectangle, then drag inside it to move everything in it.
- Brush size for paint, erase and blocked, and zoom from 10 % to 800 % on a longer slider.
- Isometric levels with 32 × 16 tiles, exported as Godot isometric tiles with diamond collision.
- Mirrored and turned tiles, also in the Godot export.
- Add several tile pictures at once (one tileset each), "Whole picture is one tile" for single objects, and pixels that do not fit the grid are left out quietly instead of looking like an error.

**Also**
- Texture Paint: small textures (64 to 256 px) with hard pixels on the model.
- The user guide has new pages: a pixel art crash course, sprites across your project, and from tiles to a Godot level.
