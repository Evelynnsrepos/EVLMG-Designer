## What's new in 0.15.0

Still no generative AI: every idea and every stroke is yours.

**Level Layout**
- Tilesets have their own panel at the bottom, like in Godot. The button at its top right pops it out so it floats over the level; drag it by its title and resize it from the corner.
- Every level in a project shares the same tilesets, so a new level tab can paint right away. Older levels move over by themselves.
- Search and sort your tilesets, and give them names, folders and colours.
- Drag over several tiles to paint them as one stamp. Fill repeats a stamp across an area.
- Layers: put fences, fountains and other see-through things on a layer above the ground, hide layers with the eye, and get one TileMap layer per layer in the Godot scene.
- Adding a tileset opens an import window that shows the picture cut into tiles: pick a common tile size or type your own, set margin and spacing, and see leftover pixels in red. Grid opens it again later.
- Black is see-through, per tileset, for pictures drawn on a black background.
- Replace swaps in a new version of a tileset picture and keeps its name, folder, blocking tiles and everything painted with it.
- Painting on a blocked cell leaves it alone, lights up the blocked cells and says why. Tick Paint over blocked to paint there anyway; blocking only tells Godot where players cannot walk.
- Pixel art stays sharp: whole screen pixels per tile pixel. Turn off Sharp pixel art in Settings to smooth painted tilesets instead.
- Shortcuts: B paint, R room, G fill, E erase, N note, X blocked.

**Keyboard shortcuts**
- Settings has a Keyboard shortcuts list: click Change and press the new key, None to clear it, or reset it to the default. It covers the app-wide keys, the board tools, Level Layout and Draw, and warns when a key is used twice.
- The user guide (F1) has a Keyboard shortcuts page right under Getting started, with the default key and yours side by side and a button that takes you to Settings.
